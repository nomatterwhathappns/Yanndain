# Agreed direction

The portfolio serves job seeking and learning documentation. Identity is Yanndain plus a real-name placeholder. Supporting content is available in Indonesian and English. All headings remain English in both modes, including card titles, page titles and article subheadings. Wording is short and calm.

The visual direction is a realistic, cinematic ocean with scroll-driven descent. The original shell and technical diagram decoration are removed. Tiny decorative labels, section numbers, depth measurements, specimen numbers, coordinates, and the vertical depth scale are removed. Meaningful metadata such as a project's category or a genuine article date can remain.

## Formations

The curiosity quote keeps its own connected transition space:

> Stay  
> curious.

500 sardines converge into an organic vortex around the words and continue swimming along the ring. Nonparticipating fish continue in other layers. Continuing down dissolves the vortex into ordinary schooling before the next section.

The horizon title is “Keep exploring.” It uses two continuous streams and a receding corridor. A manta moves toward the distant opening. Continuing down breaks the formation again on the way to Contact.

Both follow native scroll: free swimming → gathering → formation → dispersal. Reverse scrolling reverses the formation stages. No scroll capture, mandatory waiting, or forced snapping is used.

## Swimming and mobile composition

All fish swim autonomously on continuous routes. Scroll blends the formation targets; it does not supply swimming time. Sharks use a dedicated whole-body mesh. Mantas glide on their own routes with visible wing strokes. The full population remains 900.

On mobile, schools occupy multiple vertical and depth layers. Preserve title readability by moving fish farther away along the view ray, rather than pushing them all below the text.

The grey seafloor was removed at the user’s request. The background is open water with light and haze.

Small fish have fuller body cross-sections so they retain visible volume when turning. Their eyes, fin roots and light organs follow the curved body surface. The seam along the back shares smooth normals.
